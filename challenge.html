The Challenge

Understanding the crisis of flooding and waterborne diseases in Zambia's informal settlements

A Crisis of Water and Health

The intersection of climate change, urbanization, and exclusion creates devastating consequences.

The Reality in Kabanana

Kabanana, one of Lusaka's largest informal settlements, is home to thousands of families who face devastating flooding every rainy season. Without engineered drainage systems, roads and homes turn into rivers, creating breeding grounds for deadly diseases.

The community has been systematically excluded from urban planning and infrastructure development, leaving them vulnerable to climate impacts that wealthier neighborhoods are protected from.

Systemic Root Causes

The flooding crisis is not accidental—it's the result of interconnected systemic issues.

Urban Exclusion

Informal settlements are often excluded from official city planning, leaving them without basic infrastructure like drainage, sewage systems, and paved roads.

Poor Waste Management

Lack of formal waste collection leads to garbage accumulation, which blocks natural water flow and exacerbates flooding during heavy rains.

Climate Change

Intensifying rainfall patterns and more frequent extreme weather events overwhelm communities that lack adaptive infrastructure.

Rapid Urbanization

Unplanned expansion of informal settlements outpaces infrastructure development, creating new vulnerable areas each year.

The Human Cost

Flooding creates a perfect storm for waterborne diseases to spread rapidly.

🦠

Cholera Outbreaks

Stagnant floodwater contaminated with sewage becomes a breeding ground for cholera, causing deadly outbreaks during rainy seasons.

🏥

Overwhelmed Clinics

Local health facilities are overwhelmed during outbreaks, struggling to treat the surge in patients with limited resources.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Children at Risk

Children are most vulnerable to waterborne diseases, with malnutrition compounding the risks and making recovery more difficult.

Voices from the Community

Real stories from residents who live with this crisis every day.

"Every rainy season, I worry about my children getting sick. The water comes into our home and brings disease with it. We can't afford to get sick—the medical bills are too high, and missing work means no food on the table."

— Mary, Kabanana resident and mother of three

"I've lived in Kabanana for 20 years. Every year it gets worse. The floods are deeper, the diseases spread faster. We feel forgotten by the city, but we are human beings who deserve to live in dignity."

— Joseph, Kabanana community elder
